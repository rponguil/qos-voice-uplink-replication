# Figure captions (draft, English; exploratory figures marked)

Fig. 0 (paper_fig0_topologia.pdf/.png). Emulated testbed (GNS3 3.0.6, single host). Three Docker endpoints (voice, work data and residential) reach, through an 802.1Q switch, one of three border routers in parallel; only one branch carries traffic in each run: the Cisco IOS router emulated with IOU (two access interfaces; hierarchical policy with a parent shaper at R = 0.95·L), the Linux router with tc, or the c7200 under Dynamips (E4(b) only, dashed). The modem node models the uplink with netem at rate L and a 64-packet queue. Circles: timestamps (send time written in the probe payload, kernel receive time at the sink; one host clock); squares: packet captures; diamonds: queue and policy counters.

Fig. 1 (paper_fig1_E1_k3_p99.png). Voice one-way delay p99 against line capacity with three saturated competing classes (E1, k = 3, one call, m = 0.2). Lines: median of 17 runs; bands: interquartile range (IQR bands are narrower than the markers in most cells). Solid: Cisco IOS (IOU); dashed: Linux. Both axes logarithmic. LLQ, FIFO-C and CBWFQ were not run at 10 Mbit/s (IOU input limit).

Fig. 2 (paper_fig2_H3_cota.png). CBWFQ voice p99 (cell median of 17 runs) against the weighted-fair-queuing bound of RFC 2212, 2·T_r + T_s, for k = 1, 2 and 3 competing classes. Points above the dashed line exceed the bound.

Fig. 3 (paper_fig3_MOS_buffer.png). Estimated MOS (E-model, ITU-T G.107 with G.711 parameters of G.113) against the static playout buffer, at 1 Mbit/s with k = 3 and a rest-of-path delay of 100 ms, for Bpl = 4.3 (no packet-loss concealment, solid) and Bpl = 25.1 (concealment, dashed); median of 17 runs; where both curves give the same value, one label is shown. Values below 1.0 produced by the G.107 polynomial for small R are shown as 1.0. CBWFQ stays at or below 1.8 with buffers up to 60 ms and reaches 3.0-3.5 only with a 100-ms buffer and concealment; LLQ and CAKE stay at 4.2-4.4 in all 18 combinations (buffer 40/60/100 ms, rest-of-path 50/100/150 ms, Bpl 4.3/25.1).

Fig. 4 (paper_fig4_H4_forest.png). H4: difference in voice p99 between each Linux policy and LLQ (IOU), mean over 17 paired runs with 90 % CI. Grey band: equivalence margin ±MEI (3.02 ms at 0.5 Mbit/s, 2 ms elsewhere). Filled marker: equivalent (TOST, Holm-adjusted p < 0.05); hollow marker: not shown equivalent. Values beyond the axis are printed at the right edge (with the CI when it reaches the plotted range: fq_codel, E2, 5 Mbit/s, 590 B, whose mean is driven by one run with a voice collapse). Negative values mean lower delay than LLQ; part of that difference may be platform-specific (Section V-E).

Fig. 5 (paper_fig5_m_star.png). Voice p99 against the voice over-reservation m (B_v = 85.6·(1 + m) kbit/s), k = 3, one panel per capacity; median of 17 runs and interquartile band; x axis in log(1 + m). Dotted lines: illustrative budgets of 20, 50 and 100 ms. m = 3 is infeasible at 0.5 Mbit/s.

Fig. 6 (paper_fig6_colapso.png). Per-packet one-way delay of voice and of the residential UDP flow, unmarked traffic under CBWFQ (class-default fair-queue), 2 Mbit/s, k = 3: (a) a typical run; (b) a run in which voice follows the residential flow (exploratory).
